## 1. The setup (settled)

LSV dynamics
dX_t/X_t = L(t, X_t) sqrt(V_t) dW_t, with V a stochastic variance factor (Heston or similar), d<W,B> = rho dt.

Gyongy mimicking: the Markovian projection has local variance L^2(t,x) E[V_t | X_t = x]. Matching Dupire gives the calibration condition

L(t,x) = sigma_Dup(t,x) / sqrt( E[V_t | X_t = x] ).

The conditional expectation is taken under the law generated by L itself, so this is McKean-Vlasov. Define the self-consistency map

Phi: L |-> sigma_Dup / sqrt( E^L[V_t | X_t = .] ).

A calibrated model is a fixed point of Phi.

**Core methodological idea**: E[V|X] is the L2 projection: the minimiser of E[(V - f(X))^2] over measurable f. So train a network with squared loss on simulated (X, V) pairs and read off the conditional expectation. No kernels, no bandwidth, no density estimation. This is the substantive replacement for the Nadaraya-Watson step in Guyon-Henry-Labordere.

## 2. The two schemes (settled)

### Explicit (per-slice)
- One network per time slice: f_{theta_i}(x) ~ E[V_{t_i} | X_{t_i} = x], trained only on the particle cloud at t_i.
- L(t_i, .) formed pointwise, then step particles to t_{i+1}.
- Causal forward recursion, single pass. **Contains NO fixed point**: L(t_i,.) depends only on L(t_j,.), j < i. The discretised problem is solved by construction.
- Consequence for the writing: do not present the fixed-point theory as common ground for both schemes. The existence question only bites for the implicit scheme. State this asymmetry early; a referee will notice.

### Implicit (global)
- One network f_theta(t,x) for the whole surface, trained on all (t_i, X^j, V^j) pooled.
- Outer damped iteration: L^{n+1} = (1 - alpha) L^n + alpha Phi(L^n).
- Why "implicit" is the right word, but for a subtle reason: shared parameters let data at t_j > t_i influence L(t_i, .). Information flows backward through theta. That is the actual implicitness (not the time-stepping).
- Practical argument for it: sharing across t borrows strength into data-poor slices (tails, short maturities).

## 3. Theory sketch for the implicit scheme (agreed 2026-08-31)

### Damping algebra (correct, keep)
Linearise Phi_alpha = (1-alpha)Id + alpha*Phi at fixed point L*. Eigenvalue lambda of DPhi(L*) maps to

mu = (1 - alpha) + alpha*lambda = 1 + alpha(lambda - 1).

|mu| < 1 for small alpha iff Re(lambda) < 1, with per-eigenvalue threshold

alpha < 2(1 - Re lambda) / |1 - lambda|^2,

overall threshold = infimum over spectrum.

**Named assumption (the real content)**: spectrum of DPhi(L*) lies in {Re lambda < 1}. Damping cannot rescue Re lambda >= 1 (real lambda > 1 gives mu > 1 for all alpha > 0). Justify heuristically/numerically: observed oscillatory overshoot of the undamped iteration suggests eigenvalues with negative real part, exactly the regime where damping helps most.

**Damping preserves the fixed-point set**: Phi_alpha(L) = L iff Phi(L) = L. Damping buys convergence, never existence. Existence must come from elsewhere.

### Two separate routes (do not conflate)
- **Ostrowski / spectral radius**: ASSUMING a fixed point exists and Phi is Frechet differentiable there, rho(DPhi_alpha(L*)) < 1 gives local linear convergence (Gelfand equivalent-norm argument; in function space say "spectrum" not "eigenvalues"). Local convergence theorem, not existence.
- **Banach**: existence + uniqueness needs Phi_alpha to be a contraction on a complete metric space it maps into itself. In Hilbert norm the sharp linear-level condition is on the NUMERICAL RANGE of DPhi (contains spectrum, generally bigger) in {Re z < 1} plus a bound on ||DPhi||. Stronger hypothesis, global conclusion.
- Honest fallback structure: existence by short-horizon contraction (Lipschitz const of Phi ~ C*T, matching short-time literature) or Schauder/compactness for the discretised problem (existence without uniqueness), THEN Ostrowski for local convergence of the damped iteration.

### The hard technical step: Frechet differentiability of Phi
Phi is L |-> law of (X,V) |-> conditional expectation |-> sigma_Dup/sqrt(.).
E[V|X=x] = (integral of v p(x,v) dv) / p(x): differentiating a ratio of densities w.r.t. an SDE coefficient. Needs:
(a) existence + smoothness of the joint density,
(b) strictly positive lower bound on the X-marginal where L is evaluated,
(c) L |-> p^L differentiable (Malliavin calculus or parabolic PDE perturbation theory).
Note the pleasing alignment: the denominator lower bound is the tail-degeneracy issue from practice showing up in the theory. Capping/truncating the domain is not a hack, it is what makes Phi differentiable on a manageable set. Say this in the paper.

### The implementable theorem
We iterate the perturbed map Phi_hat = Phi + eps (NN regression error + finite particles). Statement to aim for: under the spectral assumption, the damped iteration on Phi_hat converges to an eps/(1-r)-neighbourhood of L*, r = contraction factor of the linearised damped map. This is the theorem experiments can verify. Same structure as deep BSDE analyses.

### Where the literature stands (from papers/READING_LIST.md)
Well-posedness of the calibrated McKean-Vlasov equation is OPEN in full generality. Milestones:
- Abergel-Tachet 2010: short-time existence (nonlinear PIDE).
- Jourdain-Zhou: existence, finite-state volatility.
- Lacker-Shkolnikov-Zhang 2019: inverting the Markovian projection, stationary solutions; restrictions.
- Djete 2022: non-regular coefficients + propagation of chaos.
- Mustapha 2024 (arXiv:2406.14074): strong existence-uniqueness, finite-state vol, propagation of chaos.
- Continuous-state Heston-LSV: unresolved without regularisation.
So: claim the fixed point for the DISCRETISED / regularised map, or under the named spectral hypothesis. Do not claim generality beyond Mustapha/LSZ.

## 4. Practical items (will bite; engineering section material)

- **Positivity**: nothing constrains f_theta >= 0 and we divide by sqrt(f_theta). Parametrise f_theta = softplus(g_theta) or learn log E[V|X]. Costs nothing, encodes a true property.
- **Tail weighting**: L2 loss is weighted by the sampling density of X_t, so tails are barely penalised, precisely where OTM leverage lives. The particle method papers over this with capping. Options to explore: importance reweighting of the loss, tail-emphasising sampling, the implicit scheme's cross-t strength borrowing. Expect most engineering effort here.
- **Dupire input**: sigma_Dup itself needs an arbitrage-free surface; cite the standard machinery (Chataigner et al. deep local vol with shape constraints is in the reading list) and treat as given.
- **Variance interpolation for explicit scheme**: L is defined on grid times; intermediate times need interpolation (as in classical particle method).

## 5. Positioning (draft)

- **vs Guyon-Henry-Labordere particle method**: replace kernel regression with NN L2 regression. No bandwidth selection; better bias-variance in data-poor regions (to be demonstrated, not asserted).

## Addendum 2026-08-31 (evening): importance sampling + warm starting

### Girsanov importance sampling for the tails (now in notes.tex sec 7)
- 2-D measure change with kernel (theta, theta_perp), constraint rho*theta + sqrt(1-rho^2)*theta_perp = 0: B untouched (V paths literally identical), W gains drift theta -> stock pushed into wings. Needs |rho| < 1. Density = explicit stochastic exponential.
- Weighted L2 with Z = dP/dQ recovers E_P[V|X] EXACTLY (abstract Bayes; ratio self-normalises pointwise). Tilt moves samples, weights fix estimand.
- Self-normalisation means only Var(Z | X=x) adds noise (from vol path; zero in deterministic-vol limit). ESS per x-bucket = tuning diagnostic for theta.
- Discrete-time: accumulate exact ratio of Gaussian step densities -> weights exact for the simulated chain.
- Defensive mixture (+theta / -theta / 0 subclouds, balance heuristic) covers both wings, avoids weight blow-up.
- Same weighting applies to NW kernel -> IS orthogonal to NN-vs-kernel comparison, keeps benchmark fair.

### Warm starting (now in notes.tex sec 8)
- Explicit: warm start theta_k from theta_{k-1}; target moves O(dt) -> few gradient steps per slice.
- Later times: update less frequently (surface approaches quasi-stationarity via V mean reversion). Caution: stale f biases L, error compounds FORWARD. Prefer event-triggered refresh (monitor regression loss on fresh cloud, retrain on threshold). Latency-accuracy trade-off plot for the paper.
- Explicit output warm-starts implicit: distil per-slice nets into global f_theta + L0 = L_explicit. KEY LINK: this constructively supplies the "sufficiently good initial guess" required by the Ostrowski local convergence theorem. Engineering trick = theorem hypothesis.

## Addendum 2026-08-31 (latency analysis, from Osian via papers session)

Concern: explicit is online (per-slice SGD inside the simulation loop) -> wants speedups. Implicit -> make it structurally offline.

### Explicit: four levers, payoff order
1. **Warm-start across slices** (already in notes sec 8; sweep confirmed unclaimed). Target moves O(dt) -> few SGD steps per slice, not a training loop per slice.
2. **Freeze the body, solve the head.** Network as feature extractor trained rarely (first slices or offline); per slice solve ONLY a linear readout by closed-form ridge least squares, O(Np + p^3), no SGD in the inner loop. Learned-feature LSMC. Per-slice cost ~ Bayer RKHS solve but with adaptive features. KEY: linear-in-features + ridge recovers the regularised structure Bayer's Wasserstein-Lipschitz theory needs -> partially answers their objection AND is the best latency/rigour trade-off. Likely the workhorse configuration.
3. **Retrain on a coarser grid than you simulate.** Kernel estimators must recompute every step; a network generalises in t. Retrain every m-th slice (or t-input net, sporadic fine-tune). Calibration identity only needs the estimator refreshed at the rate the conditional law moves. (Links to event-triggered refresh in notes sec 8.)
4. **Reduce N, not steps.** Muguruza conditioning or Cozma control variates shrink particle count per unit estimator variance -> shrinks every regression. Orthogonal, stackable.

### Implicit: make offline structural
Plain observation: the damped outer iteration is calibration-time cost, not pricing-time cost. Overnight-calibrate/intraday-price makes latency nearly irrelevant; bites only on recalibration frequency.

Intraday recalibration, two routes:
1. **Amortise the fixed point, correct online.** Offline: train map from market inputs (Dupire surface, SV params) to the FIXED POINT (L* or f*) over a distribution of surfaces. Online: evaluate operator (sub-ms), then 1-2 damped corrections to restore by-construction vanilla fit. HONEST DIFFERENTIATOR vs arXiv:2608.01217 (they amortise and stop, accepting operator error; we amortise the INITIALISATION, keep particle-method exactness). Tagline: "operator speed, particle-method accuracy." Bonus: damping analysis gives local contraction rate r -> BOUND the number of corrections needed from warm-start quality (n >= log(tol/delta0)/log r). Theory section directly powers the latency claim.
2. **Self-consistency loss** (riskier). Simulate under L_theta = sigma_Dup/sqrt(f_theta), minimise regression residual under the law f_theta itself induces, differentiate through the simulation (CKT machinery shows backprop through LSV sim workable). Fixed point becomes stationarity of one optimisation. Costs: differentiating through the McKean interaction is delicate; the clean damped-iteration theory is lost. Park unless route 1 disappoints.

### Desk combination (the story for the paper's "practice" section)
Overnight full implicit solve (possibly amortiser-initialised); intraday warm-started explicit sweeps or a couple of damped corrections on the bumped surface. Surfaces move little intraday -> warm starts near fixed point -> contraction theory QUANTIFIES convergence speed. A quantifiable latency claim, not a hope.

### B-spline head (2026-08-31, via Osian's boss)
Fixed-basis instantiation of the ridge head: cubic B-splines, knots at cloud quantiles. Banded normal equations -> O(N+p) per-slice solve, zero training. Nonnegative coefficients -> nonnegative fit by NNLS (answers van der Stoep positivity within a linear model). P-spline penalty = smoothing spline = Sobolev kernel ridge (Kimeldorf-Wahba) -> Bayer identification is classical, no lemma needed. In 1D: natural default, strongest classical baseline, cousin of Hakala's PURBF (so 1D wins over it may be marginal). Learned features earn keep in d>=2 (tensor splines blow up). C10 revised -> ablation ladder: NW -> P-spline head -> learned-feature ridge head -> full per-slice NN; headline claim becomes "spline head matches NN in 1D at lowest latency; learned features win for d>=2".

### Amortiser training data (2026-08-31, Osian's question: where do synthetic leverage functions come from?)
Three sources, increasing cleverness:
1. Bulk offline calibration: sample Heston params + surface shapes, run calibrator to convergence per sample, store (surface, params, L*). Parallel overnight; labels approximate. (What arXiv:2608.01217 effectively does.)
2. INVERT THE GENERATOR (the trick): sample L directly from a smooth positive prior (GP / spline coefficients), pair with sampled V-params, simulate that LSV model forward ONCE, price its vanilla surface by MC -> (surface, V-params, L) with L exact BY CONSTRUCTION. One simulation per sample, no fixed-point solve, exact labels, unlimited data. Caveats: (a) uniqueness = the well-posedness caveat; conditioning the map on V-params keeps it well-defined; (b) induced surface distribution may not match market surfaces -> shape the L-prior or mix with route 1.
3. Realism anchor: SSVI/SVI params fitted to historical SPX/FX surfaces + bootstrap perturbations as the market-input distribution.
Likely recipe: route 2 for volume, route 3 for realism.
Note: core experiments need NO leverage dataset (Exp B: same-Heston gives L*=1 by symmetry; Exp C validated by repricing).
