\begin{abstract}
  Scrap document recording the state of the discussion on calibrating local
  stochastic volatility models by neural $L^2$ minimisation in place of the
  kernel-based particle method. Two schemes are recorded, an explicit
  per-time-slice scheme and an implicit global scheme with damped fixed-point
  iteration, together with a sketch of the convergence analysis for the latter
  and the practical considerations identified so far.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and calibration condition}\label{sec:setup}

We consider a local stochastic volatility (LSV) model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lsv}
  \frac{dX_t}{X_t} = \Lev(t, X_t)\,\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t,
\end{equation}
where $V$ is a stochastic variance factor (Heston or similar) driven by a
Brownian motion $B$ with $d\langle W, B\rangle_t = \rho\,dt$, and
$\Lev\colon [0,T]\times\R_+ \to \R_+$ is the leverage function.

By Gy\"ongy's mimicking theorem \citep{gyongy1986}, the Markovian projection of \cref{eq:lsv} has
local variance $\Lev^2(t,x)\,\E[V_t \mid X_t = x]$. The model reprices all
vanilla options if and only if this equals the Dupire local variance \citep{dupire1994}, which forces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leverage}
  \Lev(t,x) = \frac{\sigDup(t,x)}{\sqrt{\E[V_t \mid X_t = x]}}.
\end{equation}
The conditional expectation in \cref{eq:leverage} is taken under the law
generated by $\Lev$ itself, so the calibrated SDE is nonlinear in the sense of
McKean. Define the self-consistency map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phimap}
  \Phimap\colon \Lev \longmapsto
  \frac{\sigDup}{\sqrt{\E^{\Lev}[V_t \mid X_t = \cdot\,]}},
\end{equation}
where $\E^{\Lev}$ denotes expectation under the law of the solution of
\cref{eq:lsv} with leverage $\Lev$. A calibrated model is a fixed point of
$\Phimap$.

\section{Conditional expectation by $L^2$ minimisation}\label{sec:l2}

The methodological core is the projection identity: over all measurable
functions $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l2proj}
  \E[V_t \mid X_t = \cdot\,]
  = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{f}\; \E\bigl[(V_t - f(X_t))^2\bigr].
\end{equation}
Given a simulated particle cloud $\{(X^{(i)}_t, V^{(i)}_t)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, we
train a neural network $f_\theta$ by minimising the empirical squared loss and
read off the conditional expectation. No kernel, no bandwidth selection, no
density estimation. This replaces the Nadaraya--Watson step of the Guyon--Henry-Labord\`ere particle method \citep{guyon2011smile,guyon2012particular,guyon2013nonlinear}.

\section{Explicit scheme}\label{sec:explicit}

On a time grid $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_K = T$, use one network per slice.
At $t_k$, train $f_{\theta_k}(x) \approx \E[V_{t_k} \mid X_{t_k} = x]$ on the
particle cloud at $t_k$ only, set
\[
  \Lev(t_k, x) = \frac{\sigDup(t_k, x)}{\sqrt{f_{\theta_k}(x)}},
\]
and advance the particles to $t_{k+1}$. The network takes only $x$ as input.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Explicit scheme}\label{alg:explicit}
\KwIn{Dupire surface $\sigDup$; time grid $0 = t_0 < \dots < t_K = T$;
      particle count $N$; variance dynamics; spot $X_0$, initial variance $v_0$}
\KwOut{Leverage $\Lev(t_k, \cdot)$ for $k = 0, \dots, K-1$; terminal particle cloud}
Initialise $X^{(i)}_0 = X_0$, $V^{(i)}_0 = v_0$, weights $w^{(i)} = 1$,
  and $f_{\theta_0} \equiv v_0$\;
\For{$k = 0, 1, \ldots, K-1$}{
  \If{$k \geq 1$}{
    warm start $\theta_k \leftarrow \theta_{k-1}$\;
    train $f_{\theta_k}$ by minimising
      $\sum_i w^{(i)} \bigl(V^{(i)}_{t_k} - f(X^{(i)}_{t_k})\bigr)^2$
      \tcp*{skip if refresh not triggered}
  }
  $\Lev(t_k, x) \leftarrow \sigDup(t_k, x) / \sqrt{f_{\theta_k}(x)}$\;
  advance $(X^{(i)}, V^{(i)})$ from $t_k$ to $t_{k+1}$ under $\Qm$ with
    leverage $\Lev(t_k, \cdot)$\;
  update $\ln w^{(i)} \leftarrow \ln w^{(i)}
    - \eta_k \Delta\widetilde{B}^{\perp,(i)} - \tfrac12 \eta_k^2 \Delta t$
    \tcp*{$w^{(i)} \equiv 1$ if untilted}
}
\Return{$\{\Lev(t_k, \cdot)\}_k$, $\{(X^{(i)}_T, V^{(i)}_T, w^{(i)})\}_i$}\;
\end{algorithm}

Structural observation to state early in any write-up: the explicit scheme
contains \emph{no fixed point}. Since $\Lev(t_k,\cdot)$ depends only on
$\Lev(t_j,\cdot)$ for $j < k$, the discretised problem is a causal forward
recursion solved in a single pass by construction. The existence question of
the convergence notes (\texttt{notes\_convergence.tex}) is therefore not
load-bearing for the explicit scheme; it
bites only for the implicit one.

\section{Implicit scheme}\label{sec:implicit}

A single network handles all times,
\[
  f_\theta(t, x) \approx \E[V_t \mid X_t = x],
\]
trained on the pooled data $\{(t_k, X^{(i)}_{t_k}, V^{(i)}_{t_k})\}_{i,k}$.
The leverage is updated by the damped fixed-point iteration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:damped}
  \Lev^{n+1} = (1 - \alpha)\,\Lev^{n} + \alpha\,\Phimap(\Lev^{n}),
  \qquad \alpha \in (0, 1].
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Implicit scheme with damped iteration}\label{alg:implicit}
\KwIn{Dupire surface $\sigDup$; time grid; particle count $N$; damping
      $\alpha \in (0,1]$; initial leverage $\Lev^0$ (e.g.\ the output of
      \cref{alg:explicit}); tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$}
\KwOut{Calibrated leverage $\Lev^*$}
$n \leftarrow 0$\;
\Repeat{$\norm{\Lev^{n} - \Lev^{n-1}} \leq \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$}{
  simulate full particle paths under leverage $\Lev^{n}$ (all slices,
    tilted dynamics and weights as in \cref{alg:explicit})\;
  train the global network $f_\theta(t, x)$ on the pooled data
    $\{(t_k, X^{(i)}_{t_k}, V^{(i)}_{t_k}, w^{(i)}_{t_k})\}_{i,k}$ by
    weighted $L^2$ minimisation\;
  $\Phimap(\Lev^{n})(t, x) \leftarrow \sigDup(t, x) / \sqrt{f_\theta(t, x)}$\;
  $\Lev^{n+1} \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)\,\Lev^{n} + \alpha\,\Phimap(\Lev^{n})$\;
  $n \leftarrow n + 1$\;
}
\Return{$\Lev^{n}$}\;
\end{algorithm}

The scheme is implicit for a subtle reason: shared parameters $\theta$ let data
at times $t_j > t_k$ influence $\Lev(t_k, \cdot)$, so information flows
backward through $\theta$. That, rather than the time stepping, is the actual
implicitness. A practical advantage is that sharing across $t$ borrows strength
into data-poor slices (tails, short maturities).

\section{Practical considerations}\label{sec:practical}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Positivity.} Nothing constrains $f_\theta \geq 0$ and
    \cref{eq:leverage} divides by $\sqrt{f_\theta}$. Since $V \geq 0$ implies
    $\E[V \mid X] \geq 0$, parametrise $f_\theta = \operatorname{softplus}(g_\theta)$
    or learn $\log \E[V \mid X]$; costs nothing in expressiveness and encodes a
    true property.
  \item \emph{Tail weighting.} The $L^2$ loss is weighted by the sampling
    density of $X_t$, so errors in the tails are barely penalised, precisely
    where out-of-the-money leverage lives. The classical particle method papers
    over this with capping. The principal remedy is the Girsanov importance
    sampling of \cref{sec:girsanov}; the cross-time strength borrowing of the
    implicit scheme also helps.
  \item \emph{Dupire input.} $\sigDup$ requires an arbitrage-free implied
    volatility surface; treat as given, with \citep{chataigner2020} for the standard machinery.
  \item \emph{Time interpolation (explicit scheme).} $\Lev$ is defined on grid
    times; intermediate times need interpolation, as in the classical particle
    method.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Importance sampling by change of measure}\label{sec:girsanov}

As the particles spread, few of them populate the wings, and the regression
estimate of $\E[V_t \mid X_t = x]$ degrades exactly where out-of-the-money
leverage is needed. The kernel method suffers there too. The remedy is a
Girsanov change of measure with an explicit density process, used as importance sampling, structurally as in the decoupling approach of \citet{dosreis2023}.

\subsection{A two-dimensional change that moves only the stock}

Write the volatility driver as $B = \rho W + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\, W^\perp$ with
$(W, W^\perp)$ independent Brownian motions, and assume $\abs{\rho} < 1$.
Perform a two-dimensional Girsanov change with kernel $(\theta, \theta^\perp)$
subject to the constraint
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint}
  \rho\,\theta + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\theta^\perp = 0 .
\end{equation}
Under the new measure $\Qm$, the process $B$ receives drift
$\rho\theta + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\theta^\perp = 0$ and therefore remains a
Brownian motion, so the dynamics of $V$ are unchanged, and with the same
realisation of $B$ the $V$ paths are literally identical. Meanwhile $W$
acquires drift $\theta$, so the stock particles are pushed into the wings.
The constraint \cref{eq:constraint} is one linear equation on the
two-dimensional kernel, so $\theta^\perp = -\rho\theta/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ is
forced once $\theta$ is chosen: in the $(W, W^\perp)$ coordinates the
admissible tilts are exactly the orthogonal complement of the direction
$(\rho, \sqrt{1-\rho^2})$ generating $B$, a one-parameter family indexed by
$\theta$, which may be deterministic in $t$ or state dependent. The density
process is the explicit stochastic exponential accumulated along the
simulation.

\begin{remark}[Variance cost of preserving the volatility]\label{rem:cost}
  The Girsanov exponent is driven by the full kernel norm
  \[
    \theta^2 + (\theta^\perp)^2 = \frac{\theta^2}{1-\rho^2},
  \]
  so achieving stock drift $\theta$ while leaving $V$ untouched costs
  quadratic variation $\int \theta_t^2/(1-\rho^2)\,dt$ rather than
  $\int \theta_t^2\,dt$. The volatility-preserving tilt becomes expensive as
  $\abs{\rho} \to 1$ and impossible at $\abs{\rho} = 1$, the same degeneracy
  as the existence condition, now with a rate. For equity-index correlations
  around $\rho = -0.7$ the factor is roughly $2$, material but benign; it
  should enter the effective-sample-size tuning of $\theta$ in
  \cref{rem:selfnorm}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The density process, explicitly}\label{subsec:density}

Rotate to the basis adapted to the constraint. Set
\[
  B^\perp_t \coloneqq \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,W_t - \rho\,W^\perp_t ,
\]
so that $\langle B^\perp\rangle_t = t$ and $\langle B, B^\perp\rangle_t = 0$:
the pair $(B, B^\perp)$ consists of independent $\Pm$-Brownian motions, and the
stock driver decomposes as $W = \rho B + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,B^\perp$. With
$\theta^\perp = -\rho\theta/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ forced by \cref{eq:constraint},
\[
  \theta\,dW + \theta^\perp\,dW^\perp
  = \frac{\theta}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
    \bigl(\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,dW - \rho\,dW^\perp\bigr)
  = \eta\,dB^\perp,
  \qquad
  \eta_t \coloneqq \frac{\theta_t}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}} .
\]
The constrained two-dimensional change of measure is therefore a
one-dimensional exponential tilt of $B^\perp$, the component of the stock
noise orthogonal to the volatility driver, which is the only direction the
constraint leaves available. The density process is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
  Z_t = \left.\frac{d\Qm}{d\Pm}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_t}
      = \mathcal{E}\Bigl(\int \eta\,dB^\perp\Bigr)_t
      = \exp\Bigl( \int_0^t \eta_s\,dB^\perp_s
        - \tfrac12 \int_0^t \eta_s^2\,ds \Bigr),
\end{equation}
a true martingale under the Novikov condition, automatic for bounded
$\theta$, deterministic or state dependent. Its quadratic exponent
$\int \eta_s^2\,ds = \int \theta_s^2/(1-\rho^2)\,ds$ recovers the cost of
\cref{rem:cost} directly from the derivation.

Under $\Qm$, Girsanov gives that $\widetilde{B}^\perp = B^\perp - \int \eta\,ds$
is a Brownian motion while $B$, orthogonal to the kernel, is untouched, so
$V$ is pathwise unchanged; the stock driver gains drift
$\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\eta = \theta$ and
\[
  d\ln X_t = \Bigl( -\tfrac12 \Lev^2 V_t + \Lev\sqrt{V_t}\,\theta_t \Bigr) dt
    + \Lev\sqrt{V_t}\,dW^Q_t .
\]
The importance weights, expressed in the $\Qm$-Brownian motion actually
simulated, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
  \left.\frac{d\Pm}{d\Qm}\right|_{t} = Z_t^{-1}
  = \exp\Bigl( -\int_0^t \eta_s\,d\widetilde{B}^\perp_s
    - \tfrac12 \int_0^t \eta_s^2\,ds \Bigr),
\end{equation}
so each particle's log-weight updates recursively per Euler step as
$\ln w \leftarrow \ln w - \eta\,\Delta\widetilde{B}^\perp
- \tfrac12 \eta^2 \Delta t$ with
$\Delta\widetilde{B}^\perp \sim N(0, \Delta t)$ the drawn increment. This
coincides with the ratio of Gaussian step densities
$\varphi_{N(0,\Delta t)}(\Delta B^\perp) /
\varphi_{N(\eta\Delta t,\Delta t)}(\Delta B^\perp)$, so the continuous-time
formula and the exact discrete-chain weights agree step by step: the
exactness of \cref{rem:discrete} is an identity, not an approximation
statement.

\begin{remark}[Weights are independent of the volatility path]\label{rem:indep}
  For deterministic $\eta$, $\ln Z$ is Gaussian and independent of $B$,
  hence of the entire $V$ path: the weights live purely on the stock's
  idiosyncratic noise $B^\perp$. This is the cleanest possible shape for the
  conditional-variance term in \cref{rem:selfnorm}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Weighted $L^2$ regression recovers the $\Pm$-conditional expectation}

Let $Z = d\Pm/d\Qm$. For any measurable $f$, the weighted loss
$\E_{\Qm}\!\left[ Z\,(V_t - f(X_t))^2 \right]$ is minimised pointwise by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedreg}
  f(x) = \frac{\E_{\Qm}[Z V_t \mid X_t = x]}{\E_{\Qm}[Z \mid X_t = x]}
       = \E_{\Pm}[V_t \mid X_t = x],
\end{equation}
by the abstract Bayes formula. Importance-weighted $L^2$ minimisation
therefore recovers the correct conditional expectation with no further
correction: the tilt moves where the samples live, the weights fix the
estimand. The identical weighting applies to Nadaraya--Watson regression, so
importance sampling is orthogonal to the neural-versus-kernel comparison and
improves both.

\begin{remark}[Self-normalisation]\label{rem:selfnorm}
  Importance sampling for conditional expectations is more favourable than
  for unconditional ones. The ratio structure of \cref{eq:weightedreg}
  self-normalises at each $x$, so the level of $Z$ at $x$ cancels; only the
  conditional variability of $Z$ given $X_t = x$ contributes noise. That
  variability comes from the volatility path and vanishes in the
  deterministic-volatility limit. The effective sample size per spatial
  bucket is the natural diagnostic for tuning $\theta$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact discrete-time weights]\label{rem:discrete}
  In the simulated chain no continuous-time Girsanov argument is needed:
  draw the tilted Gaussian increments under $\Qm$ and accumulate the exact
  ratio of Gaussian densities step by step. The resulting weights are exact
  for the discretised chain, with no discretisation error in the measure
  change itself.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Defensive mixtures]\label{rem:mixture}
  A single tilt direction covers one wing. Splitting the cloud into
  subpopulations with tilts $+\theta$, $-\theta$ and $0$, combined with
  balance-heuristic mixture weights, covers both wings while avoiding weight
  blow-up.
\end{remark}

\section{Warm starting and update scheduling}\label{sec:warmstart}

\subsection{Across time slices in the explicit scheme}

The target function $\E[V_{t_k} \mid X_{t_k} = \cdot\,]$ moves by $O(\Delta t)$
between adjacent slices, so initialising $\theta_k$ at $\theta_{k-1}$ leaves
only a few gradient steps of work per slice. This is a large constant-factor
reduction in training cost.

\subsection{Reduced update frequency at later times}

Early in the simulation the cloud spreads from a point mass and the
correlation structure between $X$ and $V$ builds quickly, so the conditional
expectation surface changes fast; later, mean reversion of $V$ drives it
toward quasi-stationarity and updates can be less frequent, improving
latency. A caution: a stale $f$ biases $\Lev$ at the skipped times, and this
error feeds forward through the SDE and compounds, which cuts against the
exact-calibration selling point. Prefer an event-triggered refresh: monitor
the regression loss of the current network on the fresh cloud and retrain
when it crosses a threshold. This yields a latency-accuracy trade-off with a
defensible rule, and a natural trade-off plot for the experiments.

\subsection{Explicit output as initialiser for the implicit scheme}

Run the explicit pass first and use it to initialise the implicit one:
distil the per-slice networks into the global network $f_\theta(t,x)$ as
supervised pretraining, and take $\Lev^{0} = \Lev_{\mathrm{explicit}}$. This
is more than engineering. The Ostrowski route of the convergence notes
(\texttt{notes\_convergence.tex}) delivers
only local convergence from a sufficiently good initial guess, and the
explicit pass constructively supplies one: its output differs from the fixed
point only by time-discretisation bias and regression noise. The explicit
scheme is thus the initialiser that plausibly places the damped iteration
inside its convergence basin. The engineering trick and the hypothesis of the
local convergence theorem are the same statement.

\section{Positioning: the calibration landscape}\label{sec:positioning}

Every method for LSV calibration computes, in some form, the conditional
expectation $\E[V_t \mid X_t = x]$ under the law of the model being
calibrated. The families differ in how they represent that law and that
conditional expectation. Two families dominate: particle methods and PDE
methods.

\subsection{Particle methods and their flavours}

All flavours simulate an interacting particle system and differ only in the
estimator applied to the cloud.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Kernel regression} \citep{guyon2011smile,guyon2012particular,guyon2013nonlinear}. The original: Nadaraya--Watson with a Gaussian kernel,
    bandwidth of order $S_0 N^{-1/5}$, leverage evaluated on a coarse grid
    with interpolation, kernel sums truncated for efficiency. One forward
    pass, no outer iteration. Known weaknesses: bandwidth selection, the
    kernel bias-variance trade-off, and degradation in the wings.
  \item \emph{Bin Monte Carlo} \citep{vanderstoep2014}.
    Partition the spot range into bins (equal width or equal frequency) and
    average $V$ within each bin. Notable for our purposes: their Remark~3.2
    considered $L^2$ projection onto polynomial bases, exactly least-squares
    regression with a linear function class, and rejected it because an
    unconstrained regressor of a conditional variance can go negative
    precisely where the truth approaches zero, which then divides into the
    leverage.
  \item \emph{RKHS ridge regression} \citep{bayer2022rkhs}. Penalised projection onto a reproducing kernel
    Hilbert space. The only flavour with serious theory: Wasserstein
    Lipschitz stability of the regularised conditional expectation map with
    constants of order $\lambda^{-2}$, well-posedness of the regularised
    McKean--Vlasov system at each fixed $\lambda > 0$, propagation of chaos,
    and convergence as $\lambda \to 0$ without a rate. The unregularised
    limit is explicitly open.
  \item \emph{Conditional Monte Carlo} \citep{muguruza2019}. Not regression at
    all: under piecewise-constant leverage the conditional law of the log
    spot is exactly Gaussian, so the Dirac masses are replaced by the model's
    own conditional density. No bandwidth, guaranteed variance reduction by
    Rao--Blackwellisation. Caveat in print: the algorithm fails to converge
    for large vol-of-vol, with the threshold unidentified.
  \item \emph{Machine-learning kernel flavours} \citep{hakala2019}. Benchmarks
    Nadaraya--Watson, local linear regression, RBF and partition-of-unity
    RBF for exactly our regression problem; PURBF with regularisation and
    pruning wins. Considered multilayer perceptrons and deferred them, see
    \cref{subsec:closest}.
  \item \emph{Variance-reduced flavours} \citep{cozma2019}. Additive control variates from a coupled cheaper submodel
    plus a finite-element forward solve, reported $625$-fold speed-up.
    Subtraction of a correlated baseline, not reweighting: no measure change
    anywhere in this family.
\end{enumerate}

Numerical analysis of the particle scheme itself (discretisation error,
convergence rates) is due to Reisinger and Tsianni \citep{reisinger2023,reisinger2025}.

\subsection{PDE methods}

Solve the two-dimensional forward Kolmogorov equation for the joint density
of $(X, V)$ on a grid and read the conditional expectation off the gridded
density \citep{ren2007}; the inner fixed-point iteration per time step is credited to \citet{tataru2010}, and the modern finite-volume ADI treatment is \citep{wyns2017adjoint,wyns2017fv}. Deterministic and
accurate in two dimensions; hits the curse of dimensionality beyond three
factors; the thin-density wings and the $v = 0$ boundary under Feller
violation need care. Two details matter for us. First, the inner leverage
iteration is run \emph{undamped} with a fixed small iteration count
($Q = 2$), with tail regularisation that pulls the quotient toward the mean
reversion level where the density is thin. Second, the iteration's
contraction property has never been proved anywhere in this literature: it
is asserted by \citet{reghai2006}, quoted as ``stated but not proved'' by \citet{cozma2019}, and documented to fail at large log-moneyness by \citet{saporito2019}, who replace the iteration by Tikhonov regularisation. The only
theory-adjacent entries are Anderson acceleration of the iteration with no analysis \citep{nastasi2020} and a rigorous linear-rate
theorem for the related but different Bass local volatility fixed point \citep{acciaio2024bass}. A spectral analysis of the damped leverage
iteration, as sketched in the convergence notes, therefore has no published
counterpart.

\subsection{The closest approaches to the $L^2$ route}\label{subsec:closest}

The $L^2$ projection identity \cref{eq:l2proj} is known in this literature;
what has never been done is to realise it with a trained neural network
inside the particle loop. The near-misses, in increasing order of proximity:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Linear basis, rejected on positivity} \citep[Remark~3.2]{vanderstoep2014}. Least squares onto polynomials, rejected because the
    fit can go negative under Feller violation. The positivity-preserving
    parametrisation $f_\theta = \operatorname{softplus}(g_\theta)$ answers
    this objection directly and should be presented as doing so, with
    citation.
  \item \emph{Projection identity stated, then penalised} \citep{li2023thesis}. States $\E[v_t \mid S_t = \cdot\,] = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{f
    \in L^2} \E(v_t - f(S_t))^2$ explicitly, twice, and immediately replaces
    it by RKHS ridge regression. The thesis also provides the ready-made
    experimental design: a three-way comparison of Nadaraya--Watson, RKHS
    ridge and bin Monte Carlo across particle counts, with RKHS degrading
    worst at small $N$ and under strong spot-vol correlation. We mirror that
    comparison and add the network as the fourth estimator.
  \item \emph{Global regression attempted and abandoned} \citep[p.~5]{bayer2022rkhs}. Their stated starting point was global regression on $L$
    basis functions, abandoned because the Lipschitz constants of the
    resulting conditional expectation map with respect to the particle
    distribution explode as $L \to \infty$ unless the basis is carefully
    chosen. This is the referee objection to plain neural $L^2$ regression
    already in print: our theory must either control the analogous constant
    for the network class or scope it as explicitly open.
  \item \emph{Neural networks named and deferred} \citep{hakala2019}. Frames the
    problem identically as supervised learning of $R(S) = \E(V_t \mid S_t =
    S)$ from observed pairs, rules out MLPs because ``the training is much
    more involved'', and postpones a pretrained MLP ``for potential future
    use''. The idea exists in print since 2019 and was never executed. The
    warm-starting of \cref{sec:warmstart} is the direct answer to the
    training-cost objection, and the benchmark to beat is his PURBF, not
    just Nadaraya--Watson.
\end{enumerate}

Adjacent literatures use neural $L^2$ conditional expectation regression as
standard technology (deep backward BSDE schemes \citep{hure2020}, deep Longstaff--Schwartz \citep{longstaff2001})
but nobody has ported it to the leverage loop. The structural precedent for
our importance sampling is \citet{dosreis2023}, whose decoupling
algorithm for McKean--Vlasov SDEs freezes the law component and tilts the
decoupled SDE, exactly our volatility-preserving drift, with no calibration
application anywhere in it.

\subsection{Other neural approaches, for contrast}

\citet{cuchiero2020} parametrise the leverage directly and
calibrate to option prices with generative-adversarial training; they call
the conditional expectation ``a very challenging and still open problem''
and concede no speed competition with the particle method. We solve exactly
the problem they bypass, and vanilla calibration holds by construction
modulo regression error. This is the paper a reviewer is most likely to
confuse with ours; distinguish it early. Physics-informed networks on the
Fokker--Planck equation \citep{hoshisashi2025} and neural
operators amortising the whole quotes-to-calibration map \citep{wang2026} are
different axes entirely: the former is the PDE route with a neural solver,
the latter learns across surfaces rather than within one calibration.

\subsection{Where the bar sits}

In one dimension the particle method is fast and works well, so the honest
comparison targets are PURBF and bin Monte Carlo at realistic particle
budgets, with tail accuracy under importance sampling as the
differentiator. Kernel methods genuinely fail, and networks do not, in
multi-asset LSV; if there is a dimensional story, that is the strongest
version of the paper.
