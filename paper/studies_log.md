# Follow-up studies: the estimator, the body, and the online budget

Three studies run on 10–11 September 2026 after the suite, on the six scenarios s01, s02, s05, s09, s11 and s16 (Feller ratios $1.5$, $0.7$, $0.3$, $0.1$, $0.08$, $0.17$). Every fit in these studies, and in the suite tables of section 4 of the notes, uses the full online cloud; the suite's first pass, which fitted every per-slice estimator on the earlier code default of a $30 000$-particle subsample, is kept in the store under the experiment names `suite_*_sub30k` and is quoted below only where the comparison is the point. Scripts: `scripts/estimator_grid.py`, `scripts/conv_study.py`, `scripts/budget_sweep.py`; numbers from `scripts/study_tables.py`.

## Estimator quality against the exact conditional expectation

*Setup.* For s02 (an easy surface) and s11 (Feller $0.08$), a cloud of $500 000$ particles is simulated under the PDE reference leverage of section 4 of the notes, and at the slices $t=0.25$ and $t=1$ each estimator is fitted on $(x, v^+)$ and compared with the reference's own conditional expectation $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on the resolved grid. The error is $|\hat f-f_{\mathrm{ref}}|/f_{\mathrm{ref}}$, averaged over the wings ($|x|>0.25$) or the centre. Networks are the per-slice architecture of the notes with the hidden width and depth varied, trained from a cold start on $100 000$ particles; "full batch" is Adam on the whole sample at learning rate $10^{-2}$, "minibatch" is Adam on batches of $8192$ at $10^{-3}$. NW is fitted on $30 000$, $100 000$ and $500 000$ particles of the same cloud.

*Conclusions.* (i) The $30 000$ subsample was costing real accuracy in the wings: on s02 at $t=1$ NW's wing error falls from $6.0%$ at $30$k particles to $3.5%$ at $100$k and $1.6%$ at $500$k, while on s11 at $t=0.25$ it is stuck at $17.5$, $14.6$ and $14.9%$. (ii) On the easy surface the lever is training, not architecture: width $256$ and depth $3$ gain nothing, while the same $64×2$ network on s02 at $t=1$ goes $2.6%$ at the paper's $120$ full-batch steps, $1.3%$ at $1000$ and $1.7%$ at $20 000$ minibatch steps, better than NW on five times the particles and, over both slices, $0.6$–$1.7%$ in the wings for about two and a half minutes per slice; the $64×3$ network at $20 000$ minibatch steps sits alongside it at $1.8%$ (the warm start across slices in the real pass softens the short-training penalty). (iii) On the Feller-violating scenario every estimator, NW on $500$k included, is $10$–$15%$ off the reference even at the money, and more particles do not help: the Euler cloud with full truncation at $\Delta=0.01$ is not the continuous-time density, so the reference cannot judge estimators there. (iv) On that scenario the wing fit at $t=0.25$ collapses to about $55%$ for every network trained for $120$ full-batch steps, whatever its width or depth; the $3$-layer networks escape from $1000$ full-batch steps onward ($12.8%$, and $10.4%$ at $20 000$ minibatch steps), the $2$-layer networks only at $20 000$ minibatch steps ($55.1$, $54.6$, then $12.7%$). Depth buys the escape earlier, not accuracy on easy surfaces.

## Training the body

*Setup.* The offline bodies of section 4 of the notes at $500 000$ particles were retrained on the six scenarios under two training budgets and refitted on the full online cloud: "short" is the paper's schedule ($400$ Adam steps on the first slice, $120$ on each later slice; $300$ steps per outer iteration for the implicit loop), "long" is $2000$ and $1000$ steps per slice ($1000$ per iteration on a $200 000$-point pool for the implicit loop). Each body is scored as a cold calibrator on its own overnight surface, and the lagged suite is then run on it with every fit on the full $100 000$-particle online cloud, alongside the first pass's $30$k-subsample rows.

*Conclusions.* (i) Training the explicit body ten times longer turns it from the weakest cold calibrator into the best one on these scenarios: pooled MAE $64$ bp short with $30$k fits, $70$ bp short with $100$k fits and $50$ bp long, against NW's $53$, at $2.5$ hours per body with full-batch training; the minibatch schedule of the corresponding subsection above should reach the same quality in a fraction of that and is the tuned body of section 4 of the notes. (ii) The implicit loop gets worse when trained harder ($66$, $78$ and $88$ bp along the same three arms) and its bodies are unstable across retrains; it is not a usable offline source. (iii) On the long-trained body at the full $100 000$-particle online budget the heads sit on or just under the NW re-solve under both lags: NW $49$ and $48$ bp under the surface and the surface-plus-spot lag, the RKHS head $47$ and $45$, the spline head $48$ and $49$, the ridge head $48$ and $47$, while the stale body alone is $61$ and $99$ and so remains useless after the spot move. (iv) The RKHS head of the first pass had no positivity guard and a fixed kernel width, and drove the denominator to its floor on the Feller-violating scenarios (s09: $858$ bp on the long-trained body); the guard and the adaptive width of section 4 of the notes were introduced here, and the suite's RKHS rows were recomputed with them.

## The online budget

*Setup.* The long-trained explicit body at $500 000$ particles with each head, against NW re-solved from scratch, at online budgets of $10 000$, $30 000$ and $80 000$ particles (every fit on the whole cloud), both lags, six scenarios, two seeds; the $100 000$ column is the lagged suite of the corresponding subsection above. Experiment `suite_budget`.

*Conclusions.* At $100 000$ particles every method sits within a few basis points of the floor and the comparison cannot separate them. As the online cloud thins under the surface lag, NW degrades, $49$, $50$, $52$ and $57$ bp at $100$k, $80$k, $30$k and $10$k, and the body with a head does not: the RKHS head is $47$, $47$, $48$ and $50$, the spline head $48$, $48$, $50$ and $51$. At $10 000$ particles the RKHS head is $7$ bp ahead under both lags, $9$ bp in the wings and $6$ bp at two years, and the body with either kernel head at $10$k matches NW at $80$k with eight times fewer particles; at that budget the online stage costs $1.0$ s for NW, $0.9$ s for the spline head, $2.1$ s for the RKHS head and $2.9$ s for the ridge head, against nothing for the stale body. The head is doing the work, not the body: the stale body alone is flat at $61$ bp and useless after the spot move at every budget. The ridge head is the weakest at $10$k ($54$ bp), where it refits $65$ features on the thinnest data.

## Searching the estimator recipe

*Setup.* On 12 September 2026 the per-slice estimator's recipe was searched with Optuna (experiment `optuna_offline`, study `offline_v1`, one nested MLflow run per trial). The search space: hidden width ${32,64,128}$, depth ${2,3,4}$, learning rate in $[10^{-4},10^{-2}]$, batch ${2048,8192,\text{full}}$, first-slice steps in $[500,4000]$, later-slice steps in $[100,1500]$, weight decay, fit sample ${100,250,400}×10^3$, warm start on or off, and four structural priors that can be switched on: mean matching (the fitted slice rescaled to the cloud's mean variance), a monotone penalty $\lambda E[relu(-\operatorname{sign}(\rho) \partial_x f)^2]$, a heteroscedastic loss weighted by the inverse local variance, and the continuation of the slice beyond its quantile grid (flat, linear, or the network itself). The search never sees the $20$ reported scenarios: $24$ fresh SSVI scenarios were drawn from the registry's parameter ranges, stratified so that a third have Feller ratio below $0.3$, and for each a cloud of $500 000$ particles was simulated once under the NW-calibrated leverage and its slices at $t\in{0.1,0.5,1,1.75}$ stored, split $80/20$ into a fit pool and a held-out set. A trial fits the four slices of eight scenarios in the warm-started chain and is scored by the log of its held-out squared error relative to NW's on the same particles, averaged over slices and scenarios, so that $0$ is "as good as NW" and negative is better; a median pruner stops bad trials after two scenarios and a $15$-minute cap stops slow ones. $200$ trials on four workers took three hours: $118$ complete, $73$ pruned, $9$ timed out.

*What the score can resolve.* The held-out squared error is the estimation error of $f$ plus the conditional variance of $V$ given $X$, which no estimator can remove; on these clouds the second part is $99.4%$ of the total, so NW's own estimation error is $0.6%$ of its loss and the scores cluster within a few tenths of a percent of zero. The best trials reached $-0.0016$, about $0.16%$ lower total error than NW, which is roughly $5$–$10%$ lower estimation error; converting the best trials' per-slice ratios with the estimated conditional variance gives medians of $0.92$–$0.98$ across slices. The ranking is unaffected by the constant, but the comparison is noisy at the scale of the effect, which is why the seeds are fixed per scenario and the top recipes are validated on the repricing score.

*What the search found.* Every top recipe has width $32$, minibatch training, mean matching on, the monotone penalty on (at a weight so small it acts as a tie-breaker), the linear tail, warm start on, the heteroscedastic loss off, and a fit sample of $250 000$; depth and schedule are interchangeable. The winner (trial $189$): depth $4$, batches of $8192$, learning rate $3.7×10^{-3}$, $500$ then $400$ steps, the cheapest of the finalists.

*Validation.* The top three recipes were each trained as offline bodies at $500 000$ particles on the $20$ reported scenarios and run with the spline head at $80 000$ online particles under both lags against NW re-solved at $80 000$ (pooled MAE, surface lag / surface plus spot lag): NW $44.4/44.8$, the hand-tuned body $42.8/43.5$, trial $189$ $42.4/43.0$, trial $191$ $42.7/43.2$, trial $178$ $42.4/43.1$; liquid-quote error $16.0$ for every body against $17.7/18.0$ for NW. Trial $189$ was promoted as the searched recipe (`estimators/recipes/explicit_opt.json`) and appears as "searched" in the three suite tables of the notes: as a cold calibrator it is a draw with the kernels like the hand-tuned network; as a frozen body with the head it is the best row at every budget from $30 000$ up, by half a basis point over the hand-tuned body and two over the re-solve, with the fastest online sweep.

## What the studies say

With the variance floor in place, the classical one-pass kernels are the best cold calibrators and sit a few basis points above the scheme's own floor, so at $100 000$ particles no estimator can look better than "a few basis points". A per-slice network is only competitive once it is trained properly, and even a searched recipe does not beat the kernels as a cold calibrator at $100 000$ particles: the estimator of $E[V| X]$ can be made a few percent better than NW, and that is worth basis points only through a frozen body and an online head. A frozen, properly trained body with an online head reprices today's vanillas at least as well as a fresh NW calibration at any online budget, and clearly better when the online budget is small, which is where the latency argument for the head lives. The implicit loop is not a useful offline source. The remaining distance to a $10$ bp tolerance on liquid quotes is the scheme's, not the estimator's.
