# Benchmark Suite Design

Date: 2026-09-08. Branch: `refactor-mlflow`. Companion text: `paper/notes_experiments.tex`, section 4 ("Benchmark suite"), which states the same setup for the reader of the notes and holds the TBD tables this suite fills.

## Goal

A clean, fully tracked benchmark of every particle-method leverage estimator we have, cold and lagged, with all trained objects saved to MLflow and reloadable by run id, and a section-4 table generator for the notes.

Decisions already taken with the user:

- Variance floor (`fit_v_floor=True`) becomes the package default; tiny goldens regenerated.
- 200 Euler steps for calibration and repricing everywhere.
- 100k particles for every cold row and every online stage; offline bodies at 200k and 500k.
- Two seeds (0, 1) for cold rows and online stages; one seed (0) per offline body.
- 23 scenarios: the 20 SSVI scenarios of `make_registry()` and the 3 Heston-market scenarios of `heston_registry()`. The `fig3_registry()` scenarios are not in the suite.
- Two lags per lagged cell: surface bump, and surface bump plus a +2% sticky-strike spot move.
- Section 4 tables: mean and median over scenarios and seeds, MAE first; per-scenario appendix table.

## Architecture

One new subpackage, `src/neural_particle_method/suite/`, with one module per stage, plus an MLflow experiment per stage. Every table cell is one run. Nothing in `bench/`, `calibrate/`, `estimators/` or `experiments/` changes behaviour except the floor default and two small additive hooks named below.

```
suite/
  __init__.py
  lag.py          Lag definition; lagged scenario construction for SSVI and Heston markets
  artifacts.py    save_model / load_run: trained objects to and from MLflow artifacts
  cold.py         run_cold(store, sid, algo, seed): one cold run, reusing bench.runner.run_one
  offline.py      run_offline(store, sid, body, n_particles): cached offline body
  online.py       run_online(store, sid, method, offline_n, lag, seed): one lagged run
  heads.py        RKHSHead, ridge-head wiring, and the causal online sweep
  reference.py    run_pde_floor(store, sid, seed): PDE reference repriced (attainable floor)
  grid.py         job lists for the three stages; ProcessPool driver
  tables.py       section-4 LaTeX tables and the per-scenario appendix from the store
```

MLflow experiments: `suite_cold`, `suite_offline`, `suite_lagged`, `suite_pde_floor`, plus the existing `pde_reference` (now also populated at `n_steps=200`).

## Common configuration

Defined once in `suite/grid.py` as frozen dataclasses and used by every stage:

- `SUITE_EXPLICIT = ExplicitConfig(n_steps=200, n_particles=100_000, fit_subsample=100_000, fit_v_floor=True)`; offline bodies replace `n_particles`. Every per-slice fit and every online head uses the full online cloud (decision of 10 September: the paper's 30k subsample is not used in the suite; the 30k results are kept in the store under the `*_sub30k` experiment names).
- `SUITE_IMPLICIT = ImplicitConfig(n_steps=200, n_particles=100_000, n_iters=30, alpha=0.5)`; offline bodies replace `n_particles`. Warm start from the explicit NN field (`L0`), as `bench/algos.py::_implicit_core` already does.
- `SUITE_REPRICE = RepriceConfig(n_particles=500_000, n_steps=200)`; reprice seed is `seed + 10_000`.
- Scenario `T` is 2.0 for SSVI and 1.0 for Heston markets; steps are 200 in both, so dt differs (0.01 vs 0.005). This is recorded as a parameter and stated in the notes.

## Floor default

`ExplicitConfig.fit_v_floor` default becomes `True`. `BumpConfig.fit_v_floor` default becomes `True`. The tiny golden files `tests/golden/tiny_*.json` are regenerated by the existing `tests/golden/make_tiny_goldens.py`; every file changes (one-pass algorithms directly, implicit ones through their explicit warm start). Script and regenerated files go in one commit whose message states the intentional drift and its cause. The archived replays keep replaying the pre-floor paper runs: `tests/test_golden_archived.py::replay` passes `ExplicitConfig(fit_v_floor=False)` and `tests/test_golden_warm.py` passes `BumpConfig(fit_v_floor=False)` explicitly, so those two tests pin the old behaviour and stay bit-for-bit. The `MECHANISM_ALGOS` `_vfloor` variants become redundant and are left in place.

## Stage 1: cold suite (`suite/cold.py`)

`run_cold(store, sid, algo, seed)` calls `bench.runner.run_one` with `experiment="suite_cold"`, `n_particles=100_000`, `SUITE_EXPLICIT`, `SUITE_IMPLICIT`, `SUITE_REPRICE`. It is idempotent through `run_one`'s `find_finished`.

Algorithms (registry names in `bench/algos.py`): `nw`, `explicit_nn`, `explicit_nn_tuned`, `implicit_nn`, `rkhs`, `spline`, `nw_ghl`, `bins`, `muguruza`, `purbf`. Estimator knobs are the registry defaults, except `explicit_nn_tuned`, which is `explicit_nn` with the studies' tuned architecture and schedule (hidden 64, depth 3, minibatch Adam at batches of 8192 and learning rate 1e-3, 2000/500 first/later steps) and is kept out of the golden-replayed `ALGOS` registry, in a separate `SUITE_ALGOS` dict merged into `run_algo`. The table label for `explicit_nn` becomes "Explicit NN, short training" to distinguish it from the tuned row.

Additive hook in `bench/runner.py::run_one`: a new keyword `save_model: callable | None = None`; when given, it is called with `(h, res)` after the metrics are logged so the suite can attach the trained object (below). Default `None` keeps every existing caller unchanged.

Additive hook in `bench/algos.py::CalibResult`: a new optional field `model: object = None` carrying the trained object (the implicit network, or the per-slice snapshots described under artifacts). `_implicit_core` sets it to `r.net`; `_explicit` sets it to the estimator instance. Existing consumers ignore it.

Metrics per run are those `run_one` already logs: `pooled_rmse_bp`, `pooled_mae_bp`, `wings_rmse_bp`, `wings_mae_bp`, `rmse_bp/T*`, `mae_bp/T*`, `n_failed`, timings (`fit_s`, `total_s`), and `lev_rmse` against the `pde_reference` run for the scenario at `n_steps=200`. `_leverage_error` currently finds the reference by `sid` alone; it gains the calibration `n_steps` in its lookup key so the 50-step references already in the store are not matched to 200-step runs.

Latency column of the tables: `fit_s` (calibration only).

## Stage 2: PDE floor (`suite/reference.py`)

`run_pde_floor(store, sid, seed)`:

1. `bench.reference_runs.run_reference(store, sid, n_steps=200)` (cached).
2. Download `leverage.json`, reprice with `SUITE_REPRICE` and seed `seed + 10_000`, score with `iv_metrics`, log to `suite_pde_floor` with params `{sid, seed, n_steps: 200}` and the same metric names as cold runs, `fit_s = 0`.

Run for seeds 0 and 1. The table row "PDE (attainable floor)" is the mean over both; the seed-to-seed difference is the Monte Carlo noise floor of the score and is quoted in the table caption.

The PDE x-grid half-width is checked against the scenario as `reference/pde.py` documents (`PDEResult.forward` mass check); a scenario failing the check is logged as a failed run and the row for that scenario is blank in the appendix.

## Stage 3: lag definition (`suite/lag.py`)

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Lag:
    kind: str          # "surface" | "surface_spot"
    spot_move: float   # log spot return delta; 0.0 for "surface", log(1.02) for "surface_spot"

LAGS = (Lag("surface", 0.0), Lag("surface_spot", math.log(1.02)))
```

`lagged_scenario(sc, lag) -> LaggedScenario`, a frozen dataclass with the same duck-typed interface the runners use (`sid`, `s0`, `T`, `maturities`, `dynamics`, `family`, `local_vol()`, `target_ivs(k_grid, maturities)`, `as_params()`), plus `lag` and `s0_ref` (the overnight spot).

Surface bump:

- SSVI: `calibrate.warm.scaled_bump(sc.ssvi, 1.0)` (existing; shrinks by quarters until `no_arb_ok`).
- Heston market: `bump_heston(m)` returns `HestonParams` with `v0 = (sqrt(v0)+0.01)^2`, `theta = (sqrt(theta)+0.01)^2`, `rho = min(rho+0.03, -0.05)`, `xi = 0.95*xi`, `kappa` unchanged. Model-generated surfaces are arbitrage-free for valid parameters; the function asserts `xi > 0`, `|rho| < 1`, `v0, theta > 0`.

Spot move (sticky-strike). The cloud lives in absolute log-spot (`lnx` starts at `log(s0)`; `local_vol.sigma(t, x, s0)` computes moneyness as `log(x/s0)`). Sticky-strike means the implied vol at each absolute strike `K` is unchanged when spot moves by `delta`; total variance as a function of the NEW moneyness `k' = log(K/s0_new)` is therefore the OLD surface's `w` at the OLD moneyness `k'+delta`. But the Dupire formula's explicit `k` argument must still be `k'`, not `k'+delta` -- it is not simply "the old surface, unchanged in absolute spot, with only the particles' start moved" (an earlier version of this design made exactly that mistake: F10). So:

- `LaggedScenario.s0 = sc.s0 * exp(delta)`.
- For `delta == 0` (the `"surface"` lag), `local_vol()` returns the un-lagged inner surface exactly: `SSVILocalVol(ssvi_bumped, s0_ref, T_max=T)` for the SSVI family, `DupireSurface.from_price_fn(price_fn, s0_ref, T_grid, k_grid)` for the Heston family -- both unchanged from before F10, so existing surface-lag rows in the store are unaffected.
- For `delta != 0` and the SSVI family, `local_vol()` returns `TranslatedSSVILocalVol(ssvi_bumped, s0_new, delta, T_max=T)`, whose `sigma(t, x, s0)` ignores the passed `s0`, computes `k = log(x/s0_new)`, looks up `w` and its `k`-derivatives at `k+delta` via `market.ssvi.w_and_derivs`, and evaluates `market.local_vol.dupire_local_vol(..., k)` with the unshifted `k` as its explicit argument -- the lookup and the formula's own `k` decouple under a translation.
- For `delta != 0` and the Heston family, `local_vol()` builds `w = heston_iv(k_grid + delta, T, m_bumped, s0_ref) ** 2 * T` on the (unshifted) `k_grid` for each `T` in `T_grid` (NaN-filled along strike by nearest neighbour, as `DupireSurface.from_price_fn` does), then returns `DupireSurface(T_grid, k_grid, w)`; the harness passes `lsc.s0 = s0_new` as `sigma`'s `s0`, so the surface's own moneyness lookup is already the new one.
- `target_ivs(k_grid, maturities)` evaluates the bumped surface at `k + delta` (SSVI: `implied_vol_ssvi(p_bumped, k + delta, t)`; Heston: `heston_iv(k + delta, t, m_bumped, s0_ref)`), because repricing quotes strikes as `K = s0_new * exp(k)` and the sticky-strike vol at absolute strike `K` is the old vol at moneyness `log(K/s0_ref) = k + delta`.

`as_params()` adds `lag.kind`, `lag.spot_move`, `s0_ref`, and the bumped parameters under `bumped.*`.

Unit tests: the shifted target at `k` equals the unshifted target at `k + delta`; at `delta != 0` the lagged SSVI local vol matches `dupire_local_vol(*w_and_derivs(p_bumped, k+delta, t), k)` to 1e-12 and differs from the naive "evaluate the old surface at the old, absolute spot" value by more than 1e-4; `TranslatedSSVILocalVol(p, s0, 0.0, ...)` reproduces `SSVILocalVol(p, s0, ...)` exactly; the lagged Heston local vol is a `DupireSurface` that is finite and positive on a few points, and at `delta == 0` reproduces `HestonMarketSpec.local_vol()` on the bumped market exactly; `bump_heston` on the three registry markets returns valid parameters and moves `sqrt(v0)` by exactly 0.01; `lagged_scenario` with the surface lag reproduces `bump()` on an SSVI scenario whose unshrunk bump is admissible.

## Stage 4: offline bodies (`suite/offline.py`)

`run_offline(store, sid, body, n_particles, seed=0) -> run_id`, experiment `suite_offline`, key `{sid, body, n_particles, seed, n_steps: 200}`, idempotent.

- `body == "explicit"`: `calibrate_explicit(lv, dyn, NNRegressor(seed, keep_slice_weights=True), SUITE_EXPLICIT with n_particles, ...)`. Additive hook in `NNRegressor`: `keep_slice_weights: bool = False`; when true, `fit_predict` appends `(t, {k: v.clone()} state_dict)` to `self.slice_weights` after each fit. Default false leaves the goldens untouched (no numerical change, only a copy).
- `body == "implicit"`: explicit warm start as above (weights not kept), then `calibrate_implicit(lv, dyn, SUITE_IMPLICIT with n_particles, L0=w.field, ...)`.

Artifacts (via `suite/artifacts.py::save_model`): `leverage.json` (field with `f` per slice, already the convention), `model.pt` (explicit: list of per-slice state dicts with their times, plus `Z_SCALE`; implicit: the global network state dict plus `T`), `model_meta.json` (`{"body": ..., "n_steps": 200, "n_particles": ..., "seed": ..., "sid": ..., "git_hash": ...}`). Metrics: `fit_s`, plus the cold-style scores of the body on the overnight surface (`anchor` scores) so the appendix can show how good each body was before any lag.

Sizes: 200 slices of a 1-64-64-1 network are about 4.5k floats each, under 4 MB per explicit body.

## Stage 5: artifacts and reload (`suite/artifacts.py`)

```python
def save_model(h, kind, obj, meta): ...          # kind in {"explicit_slices", "implicit_net", "field_only"}
def load_run(store, run_id) -> LoadedRun         # field: LeverageField; model: SliceBank | GlobalNet | None; params: dict
```

- `SliceBank`: times and per-slice `SliceNet` instances rebuilt from the state dicts; `f(t, lnx)` evaluates the slice in force at `t` (same "last grid time <= t" rule as `LeverageField.at`); `features(t, lnx)` returns the 64 body features plus a constant.
- `GlobalNet`: the implicit network; `f(t, lnx)` and `features(t, lnx)` as `GlobalRidge._global_features` computes them.
- Cold runs of network estimators save the same objects (`explicit_nn` as `explicit_slices`: the suite passes `knobs={"keep_slice_weights": True}` to `run_one`, which forwards it to the estimator constructor and logs it as an `estimator.*` parameter without touching the run key; `implicit_nn` as `implicit_net`). Stateless estimators save `field_only`, which is just the existing `leverage.json`; `load_run` returns `model=None` for them.

CLI: `nparticle suite load <run_id> [--out path]` prints the run's params and metrics and writes `leverage.json` and `model.pt` to `--out` (default `results/suite/<run_id>/`). Test: save a tiny body into a temporary store, `load_run` it, and check `f(t, x)` matches the in-memory network to float32 tolerance and the field replays bit-for-bit.

## Stage 6: heads (`suite/heads.py`)

All online methods share one causal sweep, `online_sweep(field0, f_stale, local_vol_S1, dyn, s0_new, T, n_steps, n_particles, head, rng) -> LeverageField`:

```
lnx = log(s0_new) for all particles; v = v0
for k in 0..n_steps-1:
    t = k*dt
    if k == 0: f_k = v0 on a one-point grid          # as calibrate_explicit
    else:
        grid = per-slice quantile grid of the cloud (0.1%..99.9%, 101 points)
        sub  = 30_000-particle subsample
        f_k  = f_stale(t, grid) + head.correction(t, sub_lnx, max(sub_v, 0) - f_stale(t, sub_lnx), grid)
    L_k = clip(sigma_S1(t, exp(grid)) / sqrt(max(f_k, 1e-4)), 0, L_max)
    advance the cloud one Euler step under L_k        # simulate.dynamics step, identical to calibrate_explicit
```

so the sweep is `calibrate_explicit` with the estimator replaced by "stale plus correction". `head=None` is the stale-f-fresh-Dupire method (no online particles needed; it just evaluates `f_stale` on `DEFAULT_GRID` per slice; its online latency is recorded as 0 s).

Heads (`correction(t, lnx, resid, grid) -> ndarray`):

- `RKHSHead(n_centres=100, lam=1e-3, width_scale=0.5, variance=None, floor_frac=0.25)`: Gaussian
  kernel ridge on the residual, kernel `variance = (width_scale * std(lnx))^2` unless a fixed
  `variance` is given; `RKHSRidge.fit_predict(t, lnx, resid, grid)`. The corrected denominator
  `f_stale + correction` is guarded to stay >= `floor_frac * f_stale` (`_guarded` helper), so the
  head can never drive the leverage to its cap on Feller-violating scenarios.
- `SplineHead(n_knots=25, lam=1.0, floor_frac=0.25)`: the same residual correction fitted with the
  penalised cubic B-spline (`estimators/spline.py::PSpline`, same knobs as the cold spline row),
  guarded with the same `_guarded` helper and floor.
- `FeatureRidgeHead(lam=1e-3)`: a per-slice refit of the body's last layer on its frozen features (64 body features plus a constant). The head minimises `sum (softplus(A w) V_SCALE - v+)^2 + lam_eff ||w - w0||^2` by Gauss-Newton to convergence (at most 20 steps) from the offline last layer `w0` (explicit: the slice network's `head` weight and bias; implicit: the global network's), with `lam_eff = lam` times the mean diagonal of the Gauss-Newton curvature at `w0`, so `lam = 1e-3` is a fraction of the data curvature and is independent of `V_SCALE` and of the cloud size. The prediction is `softplus(A_grid w) * V_SCALE`. Fitting the raw target keeps the estimate of `E[V|X]` unbiased where the truncated target `max(v,0)` has an atom at zero. When the online target equals the body's own `f` the first step is zero and the correction vanishes. Its `correction` returns `prediction - f_stale(t, grid)` so it plugs into the same sweep.

Online methods, registry `ONLINE_METHODS` in `suite/online.py`:

| name | body | head |
|---|---|---|
| `explicit_stale` | explicit | none |
| `implicit_stale` | implicit | none |
| `explicit_rkhs` | explicit | RKHSHead |
| `implicit_rkhs` | implicit | RKHSHead |
| `explicit_ridge` | explicit | RidgeHead on slice features |
| `implicit_ridge` | implicit | RidgeHead on global features |
| `explicit_spline` | explicit | SplineHead |
| `implicit_spline` | implicit | SplineHead |
| `stale_L` | either (explicit body) | none; the offline field repriced as-is on S1 |
| `nw_resolve` | none | cold NW on the lagged scenario at 100k, 200 steps |

`stale_L` and `nw_resolve` take `offline_n` as a parameter for keying only (`stale_L` uses that body; `nw_resolve` records `offline_n = 0`).

## Stage 7: online runs (`suite/online.py`)

`run_online(store, sid, method, offline_n, lag, seed) -> run_id`, experiment `suite_lagged`, key `{sid, method, offline_n, lag: lag.kind, seed, n_steps: 200}`, idempotent.

1. `lsc = lagged_scenario(full_registry()[sid], lag)`.
2. Body: `load_run(store, run_offline(store, sid, body, offline_n))` (cached; never trains inside an online run).
3. `t0`; build the intraday field per the method; `online_s = perf_counter() - t0`. Only this is timed.
4. Reprice on `lsc` (`s0 = lsc.s0`, targets `lsc.target_ivs`), `SUITE_REPRICE`, seed `seed + 10_000`; `iv_metrics`; log the cold metric names plus `online_s`, and `lev_rmse` against the PDE reference of the lagged scenario when one exists (see below).
5. Artifacts: `leverage.json`, `iv_err_bp.json`, `diagnostics.json` with the offline run id, the head parameters, and the lag.

PDE reference for lagged scenarios: `run_reference` gains an optional `scenario` argument so `suite/reference.py` can compute a reference for a `LaggedScenario`, keyed by `{sid, n_steps, lag, spot_move}` in `pde_reference`. This gives the leverage-error column for lagged rows. If the reference for a lagged scenario is absent, `lev_rmse` is omitted, as today.

## Stage 8: driver (`suite/grid.py`)

`cold_jobs()`, `offline_jobs()`, `online_jobs()`, `pde_jobs()` return job lists; `run_stage(stage, jobs=4)` runs one stage with `ProcessPoolExecutor`, pre-creating every experiment id before the pool (the known MLflow race), skipping finished runs, logging failures as failed runs and continuing. Order: `pde` (references), `cold`, `offline`, `online`. `pde_jobs()` emits both the per-seed PDE floors and one lagged reference per (scenario, lag), so every reference the online stage scores against is solved once in the `pde` stage rather than lazily and concurrently inside the online jobs. `online` refuses to start if any offline job is unfinished.

CLI: `nparticle suite run --stage {pde,cold,offline,online,all} --jobs N [--sids s01 s02] [--smoke]`. `--smoke` uses 2 scenarios, 1 seed, `n_particles=2_000`, `n_steps=10`, `n_iters=2`, reprice `20_000` paths, and separate experiment names suffixed `_smoke`, so a full pass of every stage runs in under two minutes and is the integration test.

Budget at 4 workers on this machine, from stored timings scaled to 200 steps and particle count: pde minutes; cold about 2.5 h; offline about 12 h; online under 1 h.

## Stage 9: tables (`suite/tables.py`)

`section4_tables(store, out_dir="paper/tables/")` writes:

- `suite_cold_ssvi.tex`: rows in the order NW, Explicit NN, Implicit NN, RKHS, Spline, GHL kernel, Bins, Muguruza, PU-RBF, PDE (attainable floor); columns mean and median over the 20 SSVI scenarios and 2 seeds of pooled MAE, pooled RMSE, wings MAE; median `lev_rmse`; median `fit_s`. Integers in bp, `lev_rmse` to three decimals, seconds to one decimal. Caption states the PDE seed-to-seed noise floor.
- `suite_cold_heston.tex`: same rows, the three Heston scenarios, no wings columns.
- `suite_lagged_ssvi.tex` and `suite_lagged_heston.tex`: one block per lag kind; rows `stale_L`, `nw_resolve`, then the six methods each at 200k and 500k; columns mean pooled MAE, pooled RMSE, wings MAE, `mae_bp/T0.25`, median `online_s`.
- `suite_appendix.tex`: per-scenario pooled MAE, one table per experiment, scenarios as rows and methods as columns, seeds averaged.
- `suite_cold_ssvi_price.tex` and `suite_cold_heston_price.tex`: price-space companions to the cold
  tables, same row order; columns pooled mean, pooled median, near-money, wings, `T=2` (dropped for
  Heston, which has a single one-year maturity), worst quote, from `price_bp/pooled`,
  `price_bp/wings`, `price_bp/near`, `price_bp/max`, `price_bp/T2`; one decimal, `--` when absent.
- `suite_lagged_ssvi_price.tex` and `suite_lagged_heston_price.tex`: price-space companions to the
  lagged tables, same rows and per-lag blocks; columns pooled, near-money, wings, worst quote; one
  decimal, `--` when absent.

The notes' section 4 replaces its inline TBD tabulars with `\input{tables/...}` once the tables exist; that edit is part of the table task. A method with no finished run in a cell prints `--`.

`nparticle suite tables [--out paper/tables]`.
